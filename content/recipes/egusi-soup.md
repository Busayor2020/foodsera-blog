faqs:
  - q: Can I make egusi without palm oil?
    a: Yes. Use a neutral vegetable oil and add a teaspoon of paprika for colour. It will taste lighter and less traditional, but still delicious.
  - q: Why is my egusi not forming lumps?
    a: Usually the paste was too thin or it was stirred too early. Make the paste thick, spoon it into hot oil and wait 3 to 4 minutes before turning.
  - q: How long does egusi soup keep?
    a: Up to 3 days in the fridge and 3 months in the freezer. Cool it fast and store in portions.
  - q: Can I use spinach instead of ugu?
    a: Yes. Add spinach in the last minute only. Kale also works and holds its texture better.
  - q: What is the difference between frying and boiling egusi?
    a: Frying the paste in oil gives soft lumps and a richer taste. Boiling (adding egusi to the stock) gives a smoother, grainier soup. This recipe uses frying.

This is the egusi soup I make when family is coming: rich with palm oil, full of soft melon seed lumps, and finished with fresh ugu so it stays bright green on the plate. In Yoruba homes it's ọbẹ̀ ẹ̀gúsí, and it never lasts long.

The secret is patience. Let the egusi fry in the oil without stirring so it sets into lumps, then add your stock a little at a time. Do that and you will never have grainy, watery egusi again.
